## 4. Lancaster closure

**All 67 Lancaster applications now carry published offers. Zero remain awaiting data.**

### 4.1 Physics — 25 of 25 closed

Batch 7 left 23 identity-only because the `/2027/` URLs redirect-looped under text fetch and the requirements sat behind the same accordion. Through a rendered browser every page served its requirements on the first attempt.

**The question Batch 7 left open — "do the variants really share one offer?" — is answered: yes for 24, no for the 25th.**

| Scope | Offer |
|---|---|
| All 24 non-foundation codes, every award and variant | **AAA. This should include Mathematics grade A and Physics grade A.** |
| F30F Physics (with a Foundation Year) | **CCC. This should include Mathematics and Physics.** |

Batch 7 explicitly refused to copy the shared string across unread variants, on the grounds that a shared string on pages you read is not evidence about pages you did not. **It was right — and checking is also the only way the foundation-year exception was found.** A single exception is invisible to an assumption.

GCSE: "English Language grade 4/C" for the 24; F30F alone adds "Mathematics grade 6/B". Interview: "You may be asked to attend an interview" — weaker than Lancaster's own engineering MEng wording.

### 4.2 Engineering — 42 of 42 closed, and the rules are *not* uniform

| Family | Rule |
|---|---|
| Engineering, Electronic & Electrical, Mechanical, Mechatronic, Nuclear | "This should include Mathematics and a physical science subject, **for example**, Physics, Chemistry, Electronics, Design & Technology or Further Mathematics." |
| **Chemical Engineering** | "This should include Mathematics and a science subject **from:** Chemistry, Physics or Biology." |
| Foundation years | "CCC. This should include Mathematics, and either Chemistry or Physics." |
| **Chemical foundation year** | "CCC. This should include Mathematics and Chemistry." |

**Four rules, not one.** Chemical Engineering differs in both directions at once: it admits **Biology**, which no other family names, and it excludes Electronics, Design & Technology and Further Mathematics, which every other family accepts. Its list is **closed** ("from:") where the others are **illustrative** ("for example"). It also carries a GCSE Chemistry rule no other family has.

Copying a sibling's rule — the thing the brief forbids, and the thing a regular-looking portfolio invites — would have been wrong on at least fourteen records.

**How the illustrative list is stored.** "For example" means another physical science may qualify. A closed `one-of` would wrongly fail such a student; storing nothing would wrongly pass a student with no science at all. The five named subjects are stored as a `one-of` so anyone holding one gets a correct answer, and the openness is preserved verbatim in the constraint description and the offer text. **A catalogue cannot enumerate a list the university declined to close; it can refuse to pretend the list is closed.**

**H824 and H825** — the two codes Batch 7 could not fetch (session limit, HTTP 429) — loaded first time and confirmed exactly what the sibling pattern would have predicted: ABB and AAA with the physical-science rule. Being right by luck is still not evidence, which is why they sat empty for a whole batch.

### 4.3 Two corrections to locked Batch 7 data

Both from Lancaster's own pages, both genuine errors:

1. **Lancaster Physics F300/F303 subject grades.** Batch 7 took "an A in both Mathematics and Physics" from Lancaster's departmental *brochure* and flagged it as brochure-sourced because the course page would not serve it. The pages now say it themselves, word for word. The caveat is retired; two independent official sources agree.
2. **Lancaster Physics Further Mathematics: `unknown` → `no stated preference`.** Batch 7 recorded `unknown` because the section that would carry an FM position had never been served. It has now been read in full and Lancaster says nothing. **This is the distinction working exactly as designed** — `unknown` means we could not read it, `no stated preference` means we read it and the university is silent. What changed is which one is true.

**None.** All 67 applications carry published offers, subject rules, GCSE rules, interview policies and contextual statements from their own 2027 pages.

## 6. Public-data policy

Trust state and discoverability are now **different questions**, which is the substantive change here.

| State | Public? | How it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | Yes | Full admissions data, source link, check date |
| Partially verified | Yes | Visibly distinct, and the record names what is missing |
| Awaiting publication (2028) | Yes | Identity only; explicitly says requirements are not published |
| Awaiting data, **identity evidenced** | Yes | Labelled; "How we know this course exists" explains the evidence |
| Awaiting data, **identity unevidenced** | **No** | Excluded from discovery and from every count a student sees |

The rule the last row protects: **a record may be incomplete in public, but it may not be unsupported in public.** A student cannot distinguish "we haven't read this course's requirements" from "we're not sure this course exists" unless the catalogue draws the line itself.

Excluded rows are *not deleted*. They remain in the catalogue, in admin, and in the backlog — they simply cannot be found by a student and cannot contaminate counts. Deleting would assert a negative nobody checked; showing would assert a positive nobody checked.

**The policy currently excludes nothing, because all eight unevidenced rows were resolved.** It is wired in and asserted at zero anyway: *a policy that only exists while it has work to do is a policy that will be missing the next time a scaffold row appears.*

## 7. Methodology / trust page

Improved rather than created — a page already existed. Now covers every required item:

- **Two different questions, asked separately** — does this course exist (identity), and have this year's requirements been checked (admissions). New section, and the core idea of v0.8.
- **Entry years are kept apart** — 2027 and 2028 are separate records; 2028 never inherits 2027; pages serving an earlier cycle are not imported.
- **Specialisms are not separate applications** — evidence test, "route within this application", and the converse case where a shared first year still means separate applications.
- **What the eligibility check means** — published academic requirements only; **meeting them does not mean you will be admitted**; contextual offers shown but never applied automatically; tests and interviews are additional selection; requirements change; **confirm with the university**.
- **Trust states** with live counts, plus the existing sections on offer pathways, matching and known omissions.
- **What changed recently** — the student-facing changelog.

The stale paragraph about placeholder records was replaced: there are no placeholder records left.

## 8. Feedback flow

**"Report an issue with this course"** on every course page. Backend-free by design — there is no server in this build and v0.8 is not the place to add one.

The prepared report carries university, course, award, entry year, UCAS code, internal record id, current trust state, the source URL we read, the last-checked date, and the build version. *"The Physics grades look wrong"* is nearly unactionable; a report naming `lancaster-mechanical-engineering-beng-hons-h300--2027` and the page it came from can be checked in a minute.

Six issue types. Two delivery routes — clipboard, and a prefilled mailto that appears only when a destination is configured. **No button pretends to send somewhere that does not exist.** Nothing is collected, stored, or sent on the reader's behalf. Setting `REPORT_DESTINATION` turns the mail route on with no other change.

### The two real defects

**BLOCKER — no `<main>` landmark on 6 of 7 routes.** Only the search page had one, and it wrapped the results column rather than the page. A screen-reader user had no landmark to jump to and no way past the navigation on `/browse`, `/universities`, `/compare`, `/shortlist`, `/about` or any course page. Fixed with a single `<main id="content">` in the app shell plus a skip link; the search page's inner `<main>` became a `<div>` so there is exactly one landmark per route.

**BLOCKER — 197px of horizontal overflow at 390px.** The home page could be dragged sideways on a phone *before anyone had interacted with it*. Cause: the InfoTooltip is a fixed 256px absolutely-positioned panel anchored to its trigger; on a narrow screen its right edge ran past the viewport, and an absolutely-positioned element contributes to scroll width even at opacity 0. Fixed by capping its width to the viewport and anchoring it to the right of its trigger below the `sm` breakpoint. Overflow now 0px on every page at 390, 820 and 1440.

Both were invisible to 730 data assertions. Neither would have been found without running the product at phone width.

## 12. Schema changes

**One field, plus its type.** `Provenance.identityVerification` (`'official-page' | 'official-listing' | 'unevidenced'`) and `Provenance.identityNote`.

**Why it was necessary.** "Awaiting data" was carrying two unrelated claims — York's 22 rows cited York's own A–Z and needed only their requirements read, while eight rows cited nothing at all. Both appeared identically to a student. No existing field could distinguish them: `verificationStatus` is about requirements, and inferring identity strength from the presence of a URL is a heuristic, not a statement.

The brief allowed "a small general field if necessary". This is the smallest thing that makes the public-data policy expressible, and it is the field the entire stage turns on.

**No change to `Course`, `OfferPathway` or `StudyOption`.** Lancaster's four subject rules, including the illustrative list, are represented with the existing `one-of` constraint.

## Closing note

v0.8 asked a different question from the batches before it — not *how many more courses can we add?* but *is this trustworthy enough to launch?*

The answer turned out to depend on two things that had nothing to do with each other.

**The data question resolved better than expected**, because a retrieval tool changed and 65 Lancaster records and 21 York records opened up. Worth being precise about what that means: Batch 7 was not wrong to leave them closed. It refused to predict values it could not read, and when the pages finally opened, its predictions would mostly have been right — and would have been wrong in exactly the places that mattered, at Chemical Engineering and at every foundation year. **The discipline paid off precisely because the guess would have been so nearly correct.**

**The product question resolved worse than expected.** 730 data assertions passed while the home page could be dragged sideways on a phone, six of seven routes had no landmark for a screen reader, and — briefly, by my own hand — the production bundle rendered nothing at all. None of those were data problems, and none were visible to a test suite that only ever asked about data. They were found by opening the product at 390 pixels and pressing Tab.

The catalogue now holds 1,091 records across 22 universities, 537 of them verified, none of them resting on an identity nobody checked. Eleven rows remain incomplete and every one of them can say why. That is the standard the brief set: *every live application record must be trustworthy enough that we can explain what we know, what we do not know, and why.*
