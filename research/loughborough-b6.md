## Structural surprises

1. **A stale-cache trap serves 2026 data from a 2027 URL.** Fetching a course page with no query string
   returned "October 2026" and "Fees for 2026-27"; the same URL with `?v=2` returned "September 2027".
   Every figure here was read cache-busted. See METHOD NOTE.
2. **Loughborough publishes no EPQ offer reduction** — the opposite of the common UK pattern.
   "While we do not generally include them as part of our offer conditions…"
3. **No admissions test is mentioned on any of the 28 pages** — recorded throughout as **NOT STATED**,
   never as "no admissions test".
4. **Civil Engineering does not require Physics** — `ABB including Maths` (BEng), `AAB including Maths`
   (MEng). Every other engineering course names a second required subject. Civil MEng at AAB also sits a
   full band below the A*AA MEngs.
5. **Three MEngs carry a placed-A\* rule**: Mechanical, Aeronautical and Automotive are
   `A*AA … with A* in Maths or Physics`. Recording `A*AA` alone loses a binding condition. The other six
   MEngs are AAA with no A* at all.
6. **Further Mathematics is load-bearing on 6 courses and absent on 22.** On EEE and Robotics (BEng and
   MEng) it is an accepted second subject in place of Physics; on Materials (BEng and MEng) it is one of
   six qualifying subjects. Nowhere else is it mentioned.
7. **Materials Science and Engineering is the only course that requires GCSE Maths** on its course page
   (`GCSE Maths and GCSE English Language grade 4/C`). All 26 others state English Language only.
8. **Materials names no individually mandatory A-level subject** — "two from Mathematics, Further
   Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Design Technology". Maths itself is optional.
9. **IB subject lists are narrower than A-level lists** on EEE, Robotics and Product Design Engineering.
   Mirroring one onto the other would invent acceptances.
10. **UCAS codes are not sequential or numeric.** Physics with Theoretical Physics BSc pairs F342
    (placement, longer) with F346 (3-year); Mathematics and Physics BSc pairs F340 (placement) with F341.

11. **Only one explicit "not accepted" rule exists** in the whole set (Aeronautical and Automotive MEng):

12. **Only one T Level route is published** (Robotics MEng). No other in-scope course mentions T Levels.
13. **Product Design Engineering states no portfolio requirement** — notable because a design-titled

14. **Automotive Engineering BEng omits the interview sentence** its MEng sibling carries, printing only

# PLACEMENT STRUCTURE — the answer

**Answer: at Loughborough a placement year is a SEPARATELY CODED UCAS APPLICATION.** It is not part of
the normal programme, and it is not an option selected inside a single UCAS application. But the shape
is unusual enough that stating only "separately coded" would mislead, so the full structure is below.

### What settles it

**Every one of the 28 in-scope course pages publishes two UCAS codes — one with a placement year, one
without — in the key-facts box, under one course title.** Examples, quoted with their exact duration
labels:

> Physics BSc — **F301** "4 years full-time with placement year" · **F300** "3 years full-time"
> Mechanical Engineering MEng — **H302** "5 years full-time with placement year" · **H303** "4 years full-time"
> Civil Engineering BEng — **H201** "4 years full-time with placement year" · **H200** "3 years full-time"
> Product Design Engineering BEng — **H715** "4 years full-time with placement year" · **HH1R** "3 years full-time"

Because a **non-placement code exists on every single course**, placement cannot be "part of the normal
programme that everyone does". And because the two versions carry **different UCAS codes**, the choice
is not a study option selected inside one application — the applicant picks a code in UCAS.

The course wording is uniformly permissive, never mandatory. The three formulations used:

> "All our undergraduate courses offer the option of a placement year, giving you an unforgettable
> opportunity to test drive a career and help you stand out to employers." (Physics-side pages)

> "This course includes the option to complete a paid year working in industry." (Aeronautical,
> Automotive, Chemical, Materials)

> "The majority of undergraduate degree programmes within the Wolfson School of Mechanical, Electrical
> and Manufacturing Engineering provide students with the opportunity to undertake a salaried placement
> year." (Mechanical, EEE, Product Design Engineering MEng, Robotics MEng)

> "If you choose to undertake a year in industry, you will complete a full year of assessed industrial
> training typically between years two and three of your academic studies." (Wolfson School pages)

And the university-wide placements page:

> "Whatever you choose to study, you can incorporate a placement year into your undergraduate degree."

### Per-course classification

**All 28 in-scope courses: placement = SEPARATELY CODED APPLICATION (optional, not universal).**
There is no in-scope course where placement is compulsory, and none where it is merely a study option
inside one code. The classification is uniform; only the code pairs differ (listed per course, field 2).

### The three caveats that stop this being a clean "separate course"

1. **The two codes are not two courses.** They share one page, one title, one entry-requirements panel
   and **one offer**. Loughborough publishes **no different A-level offer for the placement code** on any
   of the 28 courses. An app that models the placement code as its own course record must copy the
   identical offer, or it will invent a distinction Loughborough does not make.
2. **Loughborough does not publish whether the choice is binding.** The placements page was read in full
   and **does not state** whether a student applies for the placement version through UCAS or decides
   later, and **does not state** whether a student can switch onto or off the placement route mid-degree.
   Recorded as **NOT PUBLISHED**. Any claim that the UCAS choice locks the student in — or that it does
   not — would be invention.
3. **The placement is not guaranteed by the university.** Several pages place the burden on the student:

> "Please note that whilst the department offers great support for students to find a placement, it is
> ultimately the student's responsibility to source and secure their industrial placement."

So even the placement-coded route is a route to *seek* a placement, not a guarantee of one.

### Distinguish from genuine study options inside one application

These **are** options inside a single UCAS code and must not become separate course records:

- **Year abroad / study abroad** — "This course comes with the option to study abroad for a year",
  yielding a **Diploma in International Studies (DIntS)**.
- **UNITECH scheme** — competitive, MEng students (Mechanical, Automotive, Civil, EEE, Materials,
  Aeronautical MEng pages).
- **Engineering Physics streams** — materials engineering, electrical engineering, mechanical and
  manufacturing engineering, and **Systems Engineering**.
- **Chemical Engineering MEng specialisms** — Sustainability and low-carbon technologies; 21st Century
  Manufacturing; Biotechnology; Business and Management.

### The placement awards

Distinct from the degree, named on most pages:

> "By undertaking a year on professional placement or working in industry, you will gain an additional
> award alongside your final qualification."

**DPS** = Diploma in Professional Studies (professional placement year) · **DIS** = Diploma in
Industrial Studies (year in industry) · **DIntS** = Diploma in International Studies (year abroad).
Note DIntS attaches to the **year abroad**, which is a study option, not to the placement code.

## METHOD NOTE — a caching trap that silently serves the WRONG ENTRY YEAR

Direct `curl` to `www.lboro.ac.uk` is refused by this environment's egress proxy
(`curl: (56) CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403`; the proxy status endpoint logs
`connect_rejected … host: www.lboro.ac.uk:443`). **All** page reads were therefore done with WebFetch.

**The trap, reproduced and confirmed in this session.** Fetching
<https://www.lboro.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/courses/physics-bsc/> with **no query string** returned
a page whose start date read **"October 2026"** and whose fee heading read
**"Fees for 2026-27 (per academic year)"**. Re-fetching the *identical* page with a cache-busting
query string (`?v=2`) returned **"Start date September 2027"** and
**"Fees for 2027-28 (per academic year)"**.

That 2026 render is a stale cached copy, not a live 2026 requirements panel. Anyone reading the bare
URL would record 2026 figures and believe they were current.

**Consequence for this document:** every course figure below was read from a cache-busted URL, and
the entry year was re-confirmed on each page from that page's own "Start date" line and fee heading.
Field 20 records, per course, the year the panel actually showed.
